For grown-ups

This den is a digital companion to your child's real Lavenderbuddy® — it practises the same skills, so the bear and the app reinforce each other.

Why the funny two-sniff breath?

The breathing game is a physiological sigh: two inhales through the nose, then one long exhale. It reinflates collapsed air sacs in the lungs, offloads excess CO₂, and activates the vagus nerve — the body's built-in calm switch — shifting the nervous system into "rest and digest". Fingers tracing the stitched circles keep the exercise in the body, not the head.

Meet Gus 👋

We call the vagus nerve Gus. He's the longest nerve in the body — he sets off from behind the ear and visits every organ on his way down. When your child does slow exhales, Gus tells the heart: "all safe here, you can slow down now."

Introducing the real bear

  1. Let them lead with play. New textures, colours and the lavender scent — no agenda for a week or two.
  2. Explore together. Stroke the fabric, find the stitched breath circles with eyes closed, open the tummy pocket.
  3. Mirror joy. Hug the bear yourself, breathe, and say out loud how good it feels.
  4. Be there in big feelings. Place the bear near them and say nothing. Let them come to it.
  5. Introduce the breath. Two sniffs in, one long blow out, tracing circles with fingers.
  6. Repeat daily through play. Consistency is what builds new pathways that say "safe".
