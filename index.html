How it works

Four gentle layers, one message: you're safe

Every part of Lavenderbuddy sends the nervous system the same quiet signal — through the nose, the fingertips, the breath and the heart.

Scent

Real lavender flowers

Dried lavender inside the bear reaches the brain's calm centres in a single sniff — one of the fastest routes from the outside world to a settled body.

Touch

Tactile sensory fabric

Variable textures invite little fingers to explore. Stroking, squeezing and tracing give busy hands a soothing job to do.

Breath

Stitched breath circles

Five embroidered circles run down Buddy's tummy. Fingers trace them to guide slow breathing — no counting, no thinking, just feeling.

Connection

Social story & affirmation cards

Buddy introduces himself as a best friend who thinks your child is smart and brave. Affirmation cards grow self-worth one small truth at a time.

The science, softly

Two little sniffs in, one long blow out

The breath circles teach a physiological sigh — two inhales through the nose, then a long, slow exhale. It's the body's own reset button: it clears the short gulps of air taken in overwhelm, lowers cortisol, and switches the nervous system into rest-and-digest.

Fingers trace the circles so the exercise stays in the body, not the head — because using the brain disrupts the flow.

Meet Gus. 👋 That's our name for the vagus nerve — the long, wandering nerve that sets off from behind the ear and visits every organ. Slow exhales are how children learn to tap Gus on the shoulder and say, "we're safe now."

Getting started

Introduce Buddy in six gentle steps

Or your own way — your child sets the pace. Consistency, not perfection, is what builds new pathways that say "safe".

1

Let them lead with play

Buddy pops out of his backpack and simply joins the family. New textures, curious colours and the scent of lavender — no agenda for the first week or two.

2

Explore together

Stroke the fabric like petting a tiny puppy. Find the stitched circles with eyes closed. Count colours, open the tummy pocket, wonder what treasures fit inside. Snacks, anyone?

3

Mirror the joy

Hug Buddy yourself. Take a big breath and say out loud how good it feels. When children see you enjoying their bear, their brain tickles with curiosity and delight.

4

Be there in big feelings

When overwhelm arrives, place Buddy nearby and say nothing. Let them see you hug him. Move in for connection. Repeat — this is the foundation of safety.

5

Introduce the breath

When they're ready: a breath in on the first circle, a little sip of air on the second, and a long, slow breath out tracing down the rest. Fingers, not counting.

6

Repeat, gently, daily

Through play and practice, all the sensory signals combine into one message the brain can trust: you are safe. Sleep arrives gently, because the body feels it.

Our story

From one Mammabear, to yours

Lavenderbuddy began at home. I built it while learning the science of the brain and body — first for my own childhood journey, then for my children's specific needs. There's private knowledge stitched into this bear that made it work for them. Now it's here for your children too.

I chose the bear on purpose. For thousands of years, Indigenous nations knew the bear as Medicine Bear — a guardian, a teacher of healing and rest. The bear draws its energy inward, rests deeply, and emerges renewed. Great strength doesn't have to be spent wildly; it can be held, ordered and restored.

That's what I want children to feel in their arms: power that is safe. And it's what "Mammabear" means to me — soft and gentle in calm, fierce when my children need protecting. Both sides are part of the whole.

— Mammabear, founder of Lavenderbuddy®
